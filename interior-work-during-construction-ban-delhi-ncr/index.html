Can interior work continue during the ban? Mostly yes. Here's exactly how much.

Every winter, Delhi NCR homeowners ask us the same question in October: will the construction ban stop my renovation? The short answer is that interiors are the one category of building work that keeps moving through almost every restriction. The long answer depends on the trade, the GRAP stage, and whether your home is in Delhi or Gurugram. This guide is the rulebook our own sites follow from November to February.

01 · The Short Answer Two bans, one acronym

Delhi's winter plan is fixed. GRAP is not.

Two separate systems restrict building work in Delhi NCR every winter, and confusing them is the most common reason homeowners get wrong advice. The first is Delhi's own Winter Action Plan with fixed calendar dates. The second is CAQM's Graded Response Action Plan, which applies across the entire NCR and moves with the day's AQI.

Ban windowDates (2026–27)What stopsInterior impact
Dust-generating banNov 1 to Jan 31Demolition and outdoor civil construction that generates dustIndoor trades continue normally
Complete C&D banDec 10 to Jan 20All construction and demolition in Delhi; trucks carrying sand, cement, bricks or debris barred from entering the cityInterior finishing, plumbing and electrical work continue with dust-control norms

GRAP can override both rows

The table above is the fixed part. On top of it, CAQM can invoke GRAP Stage III or IV anywhere in NCR on any day Delhi's AQI turns severe, adding stop-work days in November, December, January and February. When GRAP is on, GRAP's list wins.

Delhi is not Gurugram

The fixed December 10 to January 20 complete ban is a Delhi government order; it does not formally apply in Gurugram, Faridabad, Noida or Ghaziabad. Those cities follow GRAP plus their own state directions, which are usually slightly less absolute but still stop dust-generating work.

The occupied-home advantage

Restrictions target dust and pollution, not habitation. Interior finishing in a home people already live in, done with sealed work zones, covered floors and no debris dumping, is the category regulators consistently permit. A raw empty shell getting its first civil work is treated as a construction site; your lived-in flat mostly is not.

The two trades that freeze

Painting, polishing and varnishing stop at GRAP III because of the compounds they release, and CAQM has clarified in a separate order that they cannot be treated as non-polluting. Tile and stone cutting stops because of dust. Everything a luxury interior needs done quietly indoors, from veneer panelling to ceiling coves, keeps moving.

Check the day, not the month

CAQM invokes and revokes stages within 24 hours; Stage III ran for about two weeks in November 2025 and was lifted the day AQI improved. Your contractor should track the daily order, not a monthly assumption. We check before scheduling any dust-generating trade, and so should anyone working on your home.

03 · The Trigger GRAP stages decoded for interiors

Four stages, and only the last two touch your home.

GRAP switches on as Delhi's AQI worsens, and since the November 2025 revision the tougher measures kick in one stage earlier than they used to. For an interior project, Stages I and II are paperwork; Stage III changes the trade list; Stage IV is the only one that can genuinely pause work.

GRAP stageDelhi AQIWhat happens to building workInterior impact
Stage I (Poor)201–300Dust-control enforcement begins: covered materials, sprinkling, site auditsNothing changes
Stage II (Very poor)301–400Strict inspections, anti-smog guns and water sprinkling at active sites, waste trucks checkedNothing changes, keep the site tidy
Stage III (Severe)401–450Earthwork, piling, demolition, masonry, RMC plants, plaster, painting, tile and stone cutting, and dust-material transport banned across NCRFit-out, joinery, ceilings, electrical, plumbing, minor MEP welding continue
Stage IV (Severe+)Above 450Essentially all construction stopped; diesel trucks barred from Delhi; only exempt public projects continuePause unless the current order permits small interior jobs in occupied homes with dust controls

Stage III is a sequencing gift

Read the banned list carefully: it removes exactly the noisy, dusty, smelly trades and leaves the craft trades standing. A well-sequenced luxury interior treats Stage III weeks as joinery-and-ceiling season and loses almost nothing.

Stage IV is rare and short

Stage IV needs AQI above 450 and typically lasts days, not weeks. Even then, CAQM's orders have consistently distinguished small interior work in occupied homes from site construction, provided dust is controlled. When in doubt, we simply stand down for those days.

Exemptions won't save a private home

Hospitals, metro, airports, defence and linear public projects get exemptions. Private flats and villas do not. Anyone telling you an exemption applies to your renovation is guessing, and guessing is how sites get sealed.

The honest fine print

Legal is not the same as fast. Four things still slow you.

Permission to work is only half the winter story. Even when every trade on your project is allowed, the season quietly taxes the schedule in four predictable ways. A realistic plan prices these in from day one.

  • Material logistics: dust-material deliveries stop at Stage III, so anything site-mixed or cement-based must arrive early
  • Fog-shortened days: November and December mornings shrink the workable window, and NCR highways slow the crews' commute
  • Cold curing: polish, adhesive, grout and sealant all take longer below about 10 degrees, even indoors
  • Workforce rotation: skilled carpenters and ceiling teams get booked early by builders shifting their own crews indoors, so October booking matters

GRAP overrides any cell

When Stage III or IV is invoked, dust-generating rows stop everywhere in NCR on that day, whatever the calendar says. The plan absorbs this because the restricted middle is built entirely from permitted trades.

Book the craft teams in October

Every competent builder in NCR moves its workforce indoors in December, and they reach for the same carpenters and ceiling teams you want. We freeze our winter crew roster by mid-October, which is why our indoor schedules hold.

Move-in dates survive

A wedding, a lease end or a school admission usually cannot shift. Tell us your hard date and we build backward from February's finishing window, because that is the one part of winter that never moves.

05 · The Playbook How Evas runs interiors through the ban

Four habits that keep a winter site moving.

Ten winters of interiors in Gurugram and Delhi apartments have distilled into a short, repeatable playbook. None of it is exotic; all of it is the difference between a project that drifts and one that hands over on time.

01

Front-load every dusty trade

Demolition, masonry, tile cutting and plaster patches are scheduled to finish before the first week of November wherever the site allows. What cannot finish early is prepped: materials counted, cut lists drawn, and every dusty task scoped so it takes the minimum possible days when its window opens.

02

Pre-order and warehouse materials

Modular units, veneer, hardware, sanitary ware and appliances are ordered in October and staged in our storage, so a Stage III order or a truck restriction never idles a carpenter. Only finished goods move in winter, and finished goods are the category that keeps moving.

03

Run the site like a clean room

Sealed work zones with zip walls, floor protection on every metre of marble and wood, covered debris bins emptied through the society's designated route, and no wet cutting on site. It is how we would treat your home anyway; in winter it is also what keeps the work legal.

04

Write the honest calendar first

Every winter-spanning project gets a schedule with the ban windows, GRAP-risk weeks and cold-curing allowances already marked, so you see the realistic handover date before you sign, not after the first stop-work order. If a date cannot survive the winter honestly, we say so in October.

What a compliant site looks like

The dust-control checklist inspectors actually check.

Every winter, enforcement teams and RWA security walk the same checklist at renovation sites. A luxury interior passes it easily, because the same habits that protect your Italian marble also keep the site legal. If your contractor cannot show these five things, the schedule is not the only thing at risk.

  • Sealed work zones with zip walls or plastic partitioning, so dust stays inside the work area
  • Floor and lift protection from the entrance to the site, not just inside it
  • All debris bagged and moved through the society's designated route and timings; nothing dumped on roads or in green areas
  • Materials stored inside the unit, never in the common area or basement
  • No wet cutting or grinding on site; stone and tile cut off-site or in a contained room with extraction

At Stage IV, work should pause unless the current order permits small interior jobs in occupied homes with controls. The deciding factor is how the work is done, not where it is done.

Finished goods such as furniture, appliances, sanitary ware, modular units and paint in sealed cans move more freely. Under the National Green Tribunal's polluter-pays directions, a violating site faces compensation of around Rs 50,000 per default plus Rs 5,000 per uncovered vehicle, and repeat violations lead to closure orders under the Environment Protection Act. Delhi additionally monitors large sites through the Dust Portal and AI-enabled cameras. A closure order hurts far more than the fine, because the site cannot restart without explicit permission. A compliant interior site has nothing to fear from inspections.

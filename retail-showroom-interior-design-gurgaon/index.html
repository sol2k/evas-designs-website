What sits inside the number

Display joinery is usually 25 to 40 percent of a fashion or electronics fit-out; lighting 10 to 15 percent; flooring 8 to 12 percent; MEP 12 to 18 percent; signage and branding 5 to 10 percent; the balance is civil finishing, paint, site management and handover. Our BOQ shows all of these separately, so you can phase spend without phasing ambition.

Mall vs high street

Mall fit-outs carry stricter material, insurance and working-hour rules plus landlord design-review cycles — typically adding 8 to 15 percent over identical high-street work. High-street stores trade that for facade rights, better visibility economics and fewer design constraints. We price both honestly and have delivered in both.

02 · What we design The selling machine

The five systems that make a showroom convert.

Square footage is identical in every quote you will receive. These five systems are what your store is actually buying.

  • Display architecture. Walls that merchandise: niches at eye level, lit shelving, hung rails, island tables and vitrines sized to your product's dimensions, not a catalogue's. We prototype one bay before manufacturing the full run.
  • Lighting design. General ambient at 300 to 500 lux, accent spots at three to five times ambient on hero products, CRI above 90 wherever colour matters — fashion, jewellery, cosmetics. Bad lighting makes expensive stock look discounted.
  • The customer path. Entry decompression zone, a power wall in the first sightline, a clockwise circulation pull where the format allows, 1.5 to 1.8 metre aisles, and the till at the natural exit point of the path. You feel this as a store that is easy to shop; it is drawn, not accidental.
  • Brand surfaces. Signature wall, cash-wrap design, trial-room experience, packaging display and facade banding — the parts customers photograph and post. We build these as joinery, not stickers.
  • The back of house. Storage per running metre of display, staff lockers, a functioning receiving path from the service corridor, and MEP loads sized for your actual equipment — the invisible 30 percent that decides whether the front of house keeps working in year three.

03 · Process Six weeks to open day

From brief to first customer, the turnkey sequence.

A retail fit-out has one immovable deadline: the lease clock. Our process is built backwards from your opening date.

01

Brief & footfall study

Product range, price architecture, target customer and the catchment's footfall pattern. We walk the location at the hours your store will actually trade before drawing a line.

02

Concept & 3D

Layout, materials, display systems and lighting rendered photorealistically, with a walkthrough video for franchise or family approvals. One display bay is prototyped at full scale before production.

03

BOQ & approvals

Line-item BOQ with named brands, then landlord or mall design review, sign-offs and work-window permits — sequenced so approvals never sit on the critical path.

04

Manufacture & fit-out

Joinery manufactured in parallel off-site while civil and MEP work runs on-site. Daily photo reports; your store manager gets a dashboard, not surprises.

05

Visual merchandising & open

Fixture installation, accent-aiming visit at night when the store's real lighting can be judged, deep clean, snag closure — and an open day on the date the lease says.

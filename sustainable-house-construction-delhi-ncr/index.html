04: Design Decided on paper, not on site

Design considerations specific to Delhi NCR homes.

Climate and bylaws in Delhi NCR reward early decisions. These are the ones that matter most, and the ones hardest to retrofit.

  • Orientation first. Living areas facing north or east with south and west walls minimised cuts direct heat gain before any equipment is bought.
  • Roof reserved for panels. A shadow-free south-facing patch sized 100 to 120 sq ft per kW, with the inverter location planned near the meter.
  • Monsoon water planned as infrastructure. Downpipes routed to a recharge pit or storage tank at the plan stage cost a fraction of post-build retrofitting.
  • Bylaw headroom. Haryana sanction drawings increasingly expect rainwater provisions, and net metering needs a structural roof certificate we prepare with the drawings.
  • Thermal envelope. AAC blocks, wall insulation, low-E glazing and ventilated cool roofs specified in the BOQ, not negotiated later.
  • Rainwater quality tiers. Roof runoff for flushing and gardening, recharge pits for aquifer return: sizing both avoids overspending on storage you will not use.
  • Gravity where possible. Overhead tanks fed by harvested water save pump hours; every hour of pumping saved is roughly 1 unit of power.
  • Summer peaking. Delhi NCR peak demand runs April to July, exactly when solar output is highest and grid tariffs bite hardest.
  • Monsoon protection. Harvested recharge relieves the waterlogging that plagues plotted sectors such as Sectors 40 to 57 during July and August.

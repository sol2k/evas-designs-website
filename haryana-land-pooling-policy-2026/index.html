Haryana Land Pooling Policy 2026: 50 per cent of saleable area for landowners.

The Haryana Cabinet has approved the Land Pooling Policy 2026, a framework for assembling land for urban and industrial projects through the voluntary participation of landowners. Under the new policy, owners will be offered 50 per cent of the total saleable area of the project, replacing the market-value-based formula of the 2022 policy. This guide explains how the policy works, what owners and buyers can expect, and where it differs from what came before.

01 — The policy What was approved

What the Land Pooling Policy 2026 says.

The essentials of the framework, in plain language.

On 3 November 2026, the Haryana Cabinet approved the Land Pooling Policy 2026. The policy sets out how a development agency, such as the Haryana Urban Development Authority (HSVP) or the Haryana Industrial and Infrastructure Development Corporation (HSIIDC), can assemble land for urban and industrial projects with the consent of the owners, rather than through compulsory acquisition.

Under the policy, landowners will be offered 50 per cent of the total saleable area of the project as per the approved layout plan. In the earlier Land Pooling Policy 2022, the allotment was calculated on the market value of undeveloped land. The remaining 50 per cent of the saleable area is retained by the development agency, which uses it to fund the internal and external infrastructure of the project.

  • Voluntary participation: an agency identifies a project area, or a landowner offers land through a registered aggregator
  • 50 per cent saleable area: the owner's share is linked to the developed project, not the raw land value
  • Public record: identified land, owners and each owner's contribution are published on the agency's website
  • Land records protected: a remark is entered in the records and registrars cannot register transactions on the land without the agency's approval
  • Consent window: owners must convey consent within 30 days of the allotment share being published

Source: this summary is based on the Cabinet approval as reported on 4 November 2026. The final gazette notification and detailed rules should be checked with the concerned department before any decision is taken.

02 — The process From notice to handover

How a land pooling project moves, step by step.

The policy defines a fixed sequence. Each stage has a published deadline and a public record.

01

Identification and public notice

A development agency identifies the project area, or a landowner voluntarily offers land through a registered aggregator. The agency must give a public notice through advertisements in four leading newspapers, its website, official notice boards, and a notification to the sarpanch or Mayor concerned.

02

In-principle approval and disclosure

Once the project receives in-principle approval, the agency publishes the details of the identified land, its owners and each owner's specific land contribution on its website. The land records carry a remark blocking transactions without agency approval.

03

Allotment share and consent

The agency publishes the land allotment share for each owner on its website and in four newspapers. Landowners must convey their consent within 30 days of publication.

04

Sale deeds within 60 days

Once final project approval is communicated to all contributing landowners, the sale deeds are executed in favour of the development agency within 60 days. Registered aggregators are paid 0.5 per cent of the agricultural collector rate after the deeds are executed.

05

Infrastructure in four years

The agency must complete infrastructure within four years of final approval. On failure, the land reverts to the owners with an additional compensation of Rs 5 lakh per acre. A one-year extension is possible with the consent of 75 per cent of owners.

What the change means

By moving from a raw land value formula to a share of the developed saleable area, the 2026 policy ties the owner's return to the outcome of the project itself. Owners carry more of the development risk, and potentially more of the upside.

What stays familiar

The basic machinery of pooling is unchanged: voluntary consent, development agencies such as HSVP and HSIIDC running projects, registered aggregators earning a fixed fee, and the 2013 acquisition law remaining the fallback for small or dissenting parcels.

What to watch

The four-year infrastructure deadline with reversion rights, the Rs 1 lakh per acre interim support and the 9 per cent interest on delayed payment are the safeguards that will matter in practice. Whether they are enforced will decide if owners see the 50 per cent share on time.

05 — What it changes For owners, buyers and builders

What the policy means on the ground.

A neutral reading of who is affected and how, around Gurugram and the rest of Haryana.

For landowners

The 50 per cent share can be worth more than a one-time cash award if the developed area appreciates, but it also means waiting through the development period. The structured interim support of Rs 1 lakh per acre per year, the 30-day consent window and the four-year reversion right are the three safeguards to track.

For plot buyers

Pooled and developed plots from agencies like HSVP come with internal infrastructure and clear layout plans, which reduces the uncertainty of buying in unapproved colonies. Buyers should still verify the status of the layout plan and the title before paying any amount.

For people building homes

Anyone planning to build on a plot received through pooling, or buying land in the new sectors around Gurugram, Manesar and Sohna, should budget for construction separately from the plot cost. A realistic per-square-foot estimate for the region is a sensible starting point.

08 — FAQ Land pooling questions

The policy, answered.

The queries landowners and buyers search for most, answered from the Cabinet approval details.

The Land Pooling Policy 2026 is a framework approved by the Haryana Cabinet for assembling land for urban and industrial projects through the voluntary participation of landowners, instead of forced acquisition. A development agency such as the HSVP or the HSIIDC identifies a project area, landowners offer their plots, and once the project is developed, owners receive 50 per cent of the total saleable area as per the approved layout plan.

Landowners are offered 50 per cent of the total saleable area of the project as per the approved layout plan. The remaining 50 per cent is retained by the development agency, which uses its share to fund roads, drainage, power, water and other infrastructure. This replaces the 2022 policy, where the allotment was calculated on the market value of undeveloped land.

The minimum size of a developed plot is 100 square metres for the residential category, 500 square metres for the institutional category and 250 square metres for the industrial category. Landowners can opt for bigger plots in proportion to their share of the pooled land.

The development agency must complete infrastructure within four years of final project approval. If it fails, the land reverts to the owners and an additional compensation of Rs 5 lakh per acre is paid to the landowner. A one-year extension is possible if 75 per cent of the contributing owners agree to it.

The core difference is how the owner's share is calculated. Under the 2022 policy, allotment was based on the market value of undeveloped land. Under the 2026 policy, owners receive 50 per cent of the total saleable area of the developed project, which links their return to the final developed value rather than the raw land value. The 2026 policy also sets minimum plot sizes, structured commercial entitlements for mid-size parcels and a defined four-year infrastructure deadline with reversion rights.

Only above certain thresholds. No commercial space is provided for pooled land under 999 square metres. For parcels of 1,000 to 1,999 square metres, one kiosk of 7.56 square metres is offered; for 2,000 to 2,999 square metres, two kiosks totalling 15.12 square metres; and for 3,000 square metres and above, one booth of 22.68 square metres. If the offered land is below 999 square metres, the owner is paid monetary compensation under the 2013 land acquisition law or the plot reserve price, whichever is higher.

Owners receive an annual interim financial support of Rs 1 lakh per acre for four years, starting from the date of sale deed registration, payable within 15 days of each due date. Any delay in payment attracts 9 per cent simple interest. Aggregators registered under the Haryana property dealers law are paid 0.5 per cent of the agricultural collector rate by the agency after the sale deeds are executed.

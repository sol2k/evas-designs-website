01 — The brief A home that hosts

One villa, two lives: family home and boutique stay.

Most stays in Gurugram split a group across hotel rooms and corridors. The owners wanted the opposite: one entire triplex where sixteen people could cook together, spread across three floors and walk back from dinner at CyberHub.

What the house had to do

DLF Phase 3 is one of Gurugram's oldest and greenest planned neighbourhoods, and the house sits in its heart, three-side open with balconies looking into the tree canopy. The brief asked for interiors that would feel luxurious in photographs, survive the wear of back-to-back guest groups, and still feel like a family home on quiet weeks. Every decision, from leather over linen on the sofas to marble over veneer on the tables, came out of that double life.

We planned the villa so every couple or family within a group gets their own floor and their own attached bathroom, while the common spaces pull everyone back together: a grand living hall on the ground floor, a family lounge on the first, a private bar for the evenings and long dining space where sixteen people can actually sit in the same room.

02 — The design Warm contemporary luxury

Wood, marble and tan leather, floor by floor.

The material palette is deliberately short: fluted wood-slat panelling, Italian marble, tan leather, beige fabric and warm light. It photographs richly, cleans easily and never dates.

The ground floor: two halls and a private bar

The main living hall wraps an entire wall in fluted wood-slat panelling, warm walnut-toned slats that run floor to ceiling and turn the room's acoustics soft and intimate. Against it sit deep L-shaped sofas in tan and cream leather around a monolithic marble centre table, under a cascading chandelier. A second, quieter lounge gives smaller groups their own corner. The private bar, finished in dark marble with brass accents, anchors the evenings. Large windows on two sides keep the greenery of DLF Phase 3 inside the room all day.

The upper floors: lounge, bedrooms and quiet

The first floor holds the family lounge, a softer room in cream and beige with curved sofas and garden-facing windows, ringed by three bedrooms. Each bedroom was given its own mood within the palette: upholstered headboards, layered lighting, work nooks for guests who arrive with laptops, and wardrobes detailed like furniture rather than storage. The top-floor bedroom is the quietest room in the house, with balcony views over the neighbourhood canopy. All six bathrooms carry the same stone-and-warm-metal language, so the house reads as one designed whole.

East-facing vs west-facing buildings — sun, heat and Vastu, explained.

Orientation changes how a home feels every single day: when the bedroom warms up, which balcony becomes unusable in May, where the winter sun helps, and how comfortable the entrance feels. This guide explains east-facing vs west-facing buildings for Indian homes, with practical design fixes and Vastu context.

For plot owners & buyers

This is for anyone comparing an east-facing vs west-facing house, flat, builder floor or villa in India — especially before buying, planning a new home, renovating, or deciding which rooms and balconies should go where.

01 — Quick answer East vs west

East-facing is usually easier in hot climates; west-facing needs smarter design.

The difference is not superstition — it is when the sun hits your walls, windows and balconies.

Factor East-facing building West-facing building
Sun timing Gets morning sun, when sunlight is usually softer and temperatures are lower. Gets afternoon and evening sun, when heat has built up through the day.
Summer comfort Generally easier to keep comfortable because the harshest afternoon sun is reduced. Can feel hotter in the afternoon and evening unless shading, glass and ventilation are planned well.
Winter comfort Morning sun can feel pleasant, but living areas may lose light later in the day. Afternoon sun can be welcome in winter, though glare and overheating can still occur.
Cooling load Often lower air-conditioning demand in hot months. Often higher cooling demand unless west walls and windows are protected.
Best for Bedrooms, breakfast areas, prayer spaces and morning-use balconies. Evening-use living areas, winter sun, views and homes designed with strong shading.
Design priority Use the morning light well and protect interiors from losing daylight later. Control heat gain, glare and fading from harsh afternoon sun.

Choose east-facing when

You want gentler mornings, lower afternoon heat and a generally easier orientation for Indian summers.

Choose west-facing when

The plot, view, road or budget makes west orientation practical — and you are willing to design for heat control.

Evas recommendation

Do not reject a west-facing home automatically. Fix the plan, shading and ventilation before judging the orientation.

02 — Sun & balconies Summer vs winter

Which balconies face the sun in summer and winter?

For most of India, the sun rises in the east, sets in the west, is higher and more northerly in summer, and lower and more southerly in winter.

Balcony direction Summer behaviour Winter behaviour Best use
East-facing balcony Morning sun; usually comfortable early, shaded later as the day heats up. Pleasant morning sun, though afternoons may feel cooler. Tea, breakfast, plants that like gentle sun, morning yoga.
West-facing balcony Strong afternoon and evening sun; can become the hottest part of the home. Useful afternoon warmth, but glare can still be uncomfortable. Evening seating with shading, screens, plants that tolerate heat.
South-facing balcony High summer sun means less deep penetration if there is a roof or chajja, but midday heat can still be strong. Often the best winter sun because the lower sun sits in the southern sky. Winter seating, drying clothes, light-loving plants with summer shading.
North-facing balcony Usually the coolest; may get brief early morning or late evening sun around peak summer. Little direct sun; can feel cold or damp in winter. Utility, reading in hot months, shade-loving plants, heat relief.

Design note: in Delhi NCR and similar latitudes, a west-facing balcony in May behaves very differently from a west-facing balcony in January. If you are planning a new home, our building architect in Gurgaon team studies orientation before fixing room positions, window sizes and balcony depths.

03 — Vastu relevance For clients who follow Vastu

Entrance, bedrooms, washrooms and kitchen — what people usually ask for.

Vastu preferences vary by family and practitioner. We treat them as planning inputs and balance them with sunlight, ventilation, structure and approvals.

Space Common Vastu preference Practical design note
Main entrance East, north or north-east are often preferred; west-facing entrances are usually handled with placement and remedies. Entrance experience also depends on approach road, privacy, security, shading and foyer proportions.
Master bedroom South-west is commonly preferred for stability. South-west can reduce harsh morning light but may need ventilation and shading control in summer.
Children’s bedroom West or north-west are often suggested. West-facing bedrooms need heat control in summer afternoons.
Kitchen South-east is the classic preference; north-west is often the second option. Heat-generating rooms should not also suffer avoidable afternoon solar gain where the plan can prevent it.
Washrooms / toilets North-west or west are commonly used; many families avoid north-east and south-west toilets. Ventilation, plumbing stacking, privacy and waterproofing matter as much as direction.
Prayer / meditation North-east is widely preferred. Soft morning light can support this beautifully when the plan allows.

FAQ

East-facing vs west-facing questions

The questions homeowners ask before buying or building.

Not always. East-facing homes usually get softer morning sun and avoid the harshest afternoon heat, which many homeowners prefer in hot Indian cities. West-facing homes can still work well when the design handles heat gain with shading, insulation, window sizing, ventilation and interior planning. Orientation matters, but layout and execution decide how comfortable the home feels.

In most of India, east-facing balconies get morning sun all year. West-facing balconies get strong afternoon and evening sun, especially in summer. South-facing balconies usually receive more useful winter sun because the winter sun is lower in the southern sky, while north-facing balconies stay the coolest and may only see brief early or late sun around peak summer.

Many Vastu traditions prefer east, north or north-east entrances, but west-facing homes are not automatically considered bad. For clients who follow Vastu, the focus usually shifts to entrance placement, room zoning, balcony use, kitchen location and remedies that balance heat and light. We treat Vastu as one design input alongside structure, ventilation, privacy and budget.

For Vastu, the master bedroom is commonly preferred in the south-west, with children’s bedrooms often placed in the west or north-west. From a sunlight point of view, west-facing bedrooms can become hot in the afternoon, while east-facing bedrooms get gentler morning light. The best result usually comes from combining Vastu zoning with shading, window placement and ventilation.

Use a combination of external shading, deeper balconies, chajjas, high-performance glass, curtains or blinds, cross-ventilation, light exterior colours and good roof or wall insulation. Inside, place heat-sensitive rooms away from the harshest west exposure where possible and use the west side for buffers such as stairs, storage or utility when the plan allows.

If Vastu is important to you, it should be discussed at the concept stage rather than forced in later. A good plan can respect major Vastu preferences while still solving sunlight, ventilation, structure, parking, approvals and family needs. The goal is not to choose between Vastu and good design, but to make them work together.

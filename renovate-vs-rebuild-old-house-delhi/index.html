Decision guide · South Delhi & NCR

Renovate or rebuild your old Delhi house? The honest decision framework.

If you own a 30 to 60 year old kothi in Greater Kailash, Saket, Vasant Vihar, Panchsheel or Malviya Nagar, you are sitting on the most consequential decision in Delhi residential real estate: pour money into the old bones, or demolish and build the house the plot deserves. Get it wrong one way and you spend Rs 80 lakh renovating a structure that deserved retirement; get it wrong the other way and you demolish a sound house and add a crore to the budget for nothing. This guide gives you the framework we use on real projects: the 2026 cost comparison per sq ft, the structural audit checklist that settles the question with evidence, the FAR and stilt-plus-four upside that changes the math on good plots, approval implications of both paths, and honest timelines. We audit, renovate and rebuild old houses across South Delhi and Gurgaon, so we have no bias to sell you either answer.

Quick answer: In 2026 Delhi, renovate when the structure is sound and the layout works: deep renovation costs Rs 1,200 to 2,800 per sq ft versus Rs 2,800 to 3,800 or more for demolition and reconstruction. Rebuild when the audit finds structural distress (sagging slabs, corroded reinforcement, foundation settlement), when the plot's unused FAR and stilt-plus-four rights make a bigger house pay for itself, or when renovation quotes creep past 60 to 70 percent of rebuild cost. A structural audit, costing a fraction of either path, is the only honest way to decide.

The 60-70 percent rule

When honest renovation quotes climb past 60 to 70 percent of reconstruction cost, renovation stops making sense: you pay near-rebuild money for a house that is still old at its core, with none of the new-house warranties, spans or services. This crossover surprises most owners, which is exactly why we quote both paths before recommending either.

The FAR upside that flips decisions

A South Delhi kothi on 250 sq yd often sits under its permitted floor area, and stilt-plus-four rights can more than double the saleable or usable area. When a rebuild unlocks a floor you can sell, rent or house family in, the reconstruction effectively pays for itself. Renovation never unlocks that.

The emotional row, honestly

Sentiment is a real cost item, not a tiebreaker. If the house holds family history, we show you what preservation renovation looks like at full honesty, including its ceiling of possibility. If the sentiment survives the numbers, renovate proudly. If it does not, rebuild without guilt.

The hidden renovation tax

Old houses surprise you mid-job: dead wiring inside walls, rusted pipes inside slabs, waterproofing that was never done. Honest renovation budgets carry a 10 to 15 percent discovery contingency, and our contracts state it before work starts, not in the third variation letter.

Timelines compared

Cosmetic renovation: 3 to 5 months, liveable in phases. Deep renovation: 5 to 8 months, usually with the family partially moved. Rebuild: 14 to 18 months end to end, family fully relocated, plus a month of demolition and approvals at the front.

03 · The audit Evidence before emotion

The structural audit checklist that settles the argument.

A structural audit is a one to two week, fixed-fee exercise that ends the renovate-or-rebuild debate with measurements instead of opinions. This is what we inspect.

  • Foundation and settlement. Differential settlement cracks at plinth and corners, soil and moisture behaviour, the single most decisive finding in the audit.
  • Columns and beams. Cover loss, exposed or corroded reinforcement, spalling, and load-path sense in houses that grew by unplanned additions.
  • Slabs. Sag measurement, deflection under load, corrosion mapping where leakage has lived for years.
  • Walls and masonry. Load-bearing versus partition behaviour, crack patterns that talk about movement, damp mapping.
  • Services spine. Whether plumbing, electrical and drainage can be renewed inside the existing geometry, or whether the shafts themselves are the problem.
  • Roof and water. Terrace waterproofing history, slope failures, and the water-ingress map that predicts future decay better than any other test.

Approval rules, both paths

  • Renovation within the sanctioned envelope needs no fresh plan sanction; structural additions do
  • Reconstruction requires a fresh sanctioned plan under current bye-laws, with setbacks and height as they stand today
  • Stilt-plus-four and purchasable FAR are assessed at sanction; this is where the rebuild upside is locked in
  • Demolition itself needs intimation and debris-management compliance in Delhi
  • Completion and occupancy certification closes the rebuild, keeping resale and finance clean

For the approval mechanics, read building plan approval in Gurgaon; the Delhi MCD and DDA process follows the same logic with its own portals.

Where each path usually wins

  • 1980s-90s kothis with sound frames and dated interiors: renovate, almost always
  • Houses with failed foundations or two generations of unplanned additions: rebuild
  • Plots with large unused FAR in premium colonies: rebuild, and let the new floor fund it
  • Houses you must keep living in: renovate in phases, or accept rented accommodation for a rebuild

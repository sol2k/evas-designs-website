01 · Zone by Zone Where your office gets judged

Five zones, in the order your guests see them.

Skip the server room. These are the spaces that decide what people think of your company in October.

01

The facade and entrance

This is your office's face to the street and the first photograph every visitor takes. Wash the building face in warm light, place brass lanterns at the door, and stop there. One garland per column. Lit façades photograph premium; cluttered ones photograph nervous.

02

The reception

Clients wait here, and waiting minds judge. One statement console, fresh orchids or marigolds, a brass urli with floating petals, and lighting at eye level that flatters faces on video calls. A tidy reception reads as a well-run company.

03

The work floors

Staff sit here fifty weeks a year, so restraint wins. One festive accent per zone: a diya tray in the break-out corner, a single marigold arrangement at the pass. Productivity drops when the floor looks like a gift shop.

04

The meeting rooms

One low centrepiece on the table, below eye level so people can talk across it. Warm dimmable light beats bright panels for every October conversation. If the room hosts festive client meetings, style it for photographs, not for clutter.

05

The cafe and break-out

Where the Diwali party happens. Layered light, grouped lanterns in a corner, one wall washed warm. This zone can carry more festive styling than anywhere else, because this is where people relax and take pictures.

Light is the first thing judged

The facade is your rangoli. Light it like you mean it.

A building can be twenty years old and look new in October light, or five years old and look tired under white tubes. Facade lighting is an electrician's job plus a designer's eye: warm temperature, washed surfaces, lanterns at human scale. It takes days, not weeks, and it is the single highest-impact move before Diwali.

  • Warm-white washes on stone, wood and entrance columns
  • Lanterns and diyas at the door, grouped in odd numbers
  • One garland per column, never a wall of orange
  • Timers so the building glows at dusk without staff effort

Inside the office

Reception styling that survives the Monday after.

The mistake most offices make is renting decoration that looks festive for one week and cheap for fifty-one. We style with pieces that stay: brass, stone, timber and fresh florals in strong vases. The office looks festive in October and quietly premium the rest of the year, which is a better use of the same budget.

  • A statement console that hides cables, keys and clutter
  • Fresh flowers replaced weekly through the festive season
  • Urli, lanterns and trays that become permanent fixtures
  • Joinery, paint and lighting fixed as one job, one vendor

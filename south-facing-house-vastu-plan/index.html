01 · The honest truth Myth vs architecture

Why south facing got a bad name — and why it is mostly outdated physics.

The fear around south facing homes is older than cement. Understanding where it came from tells you exactly how much of it still applies in a 2026 Delhi NCR home.

  • The sun was the villain, not the direction. South facing openings take the high, harsh midday sun in North India. In homes without glass, insulation or mechanical cooling, that meant hot rooms and fading fabrics — a real daily penalty two generations ago.
  • Modern glazing flips the equation. Double glazing, low-E glass, external shading and a 1.5-ton inverter now neutralise the heat gain that defined the old advice. The direction itself carries no more penalty than any other.
  • Steady, glare-free light is an asset. South light is even and shadow-soft through the day — prized in living rooms, studios and home offices because it does not blast or fade the way east and west light does.
  • Vastu never banned the south. The classical texts place Yama, lord of order and restraint, in the south and prescribe exactly how to use it well. The rules below are the prescription, not a warning.
  • The resale fear is local, not universal. In much of South India and in colder northern markets, south facing plots command premiums. In Delhi NCR the discount is a negotiation artifact — which is exactly why disciplined buyers pay attention.
02 · The entrance The rule that matters most

The pada map: where exactly the main door goes on a south wall.

Vastu divides each wall into nine equal segments, padas, from the corner. On the south wall, the auspicious entrance zone is the centre stretch — close to Yama's seat, the zone of stability.

South-wall padasLocationVerdict for the main entrance
1 to 3From the south-east corner westwardAcceptable with corrections for rental or commercial use; not preferred for a family home's main door
3 to 6The central stretch of the south wallBest zone: stability, discipline and earned prosperity. This is where the main entrance belongs
7 to 9Toward the south-west cornerAvoid for the entrance; keep this corner heavy, high and solid — it anchors the whole house

What the entrance rule is really doing

Classical Vastu assigns each pada to a deity and an energy quality. The central south padas sit in Yama's domain of order — the texts consider an entrance there steadying for the household. In design terms, a central entrance also balances frontage, gives the living room two-sided light and places the foyer away from both plot corners.

Door details that complete the rule

The main door should be the largest in the house, open clockwise, and sit slightly raised on the plinth. Nameplate at eye level, a threshold that is not cracked, and no obstruction — garbage bins, poles, thorny plants — directly facing the door. These are finish details; the pada position is decided on the plan, before any of them exist.

The south-west is the anchor

The single most repeated rule for a south facing plot: the south-west corner must be the heaviest, highest and most solid part of the house — master bedroom, load walls, overhead tank. A cut, low or empty south-west is the one defect Vastu treats as genuinely serious, and the one a good architect can always design around.

Bedroom micro-rules

Sleep with the head to the south or east — for the master in a south facing home, the headboard on the south wall is ideal. Wardrobes go on the west or south walls; mirrors avoid facing the bed. Small rules, zero cost, applied on the furniture plan in one pass.

04 · Plot rules Shape, slope and cuts

The plot itself: what to check before you fall in love with a south facing site.

Direction is one chapter of Vastu. The plot's geometry decides how much of the rulebook the architect can honour without gymnastics.

  • Shape. Square or rectangle with sides close to equal is ideal. A cut or extension in the south-east or south-west is the defect to walk away from; north and east extensions are workable with design.
  • Slope. The plot should slope down toward the north or east, and never hold standing water in the south-west after rain.
  • South-west corner. Highest point of the plot, highest part of the plinth, heaviest part of the building. If the corner is low, raise it with plinth design — cheap on paper, expensive to fix later.
  • Road position. A plot with the road on the south needs the entrance pada discipline above. Corner plots with roads on south and east or south and west get more design freedom, not less.
  • Vastu and light are allies here. South facing lets you open the longer north side to steady light — place living areas and home offices on the north facade and the house feels bright all day, which is Vastu-compliant and simply good design in the same stroke.
05 · Corrections Existing homes

Already own a south facing house? Most corrections are design moves, not demolitions.

Vastu correction has a spectrum: geometry first, elements second, rituals never. These are the corrections we actually execute on real projects.

Entrance corrections

An entrance on the wrong pada can often be shifted within the same opening or re-established with a foyer screen, shifting the effective energy line to the central zone without touching structure. A brass threshold, corrected door swing and clear approach complete it.

Weight and height corrections

An empty or low south-west gets weight: master suite relocation where possible, otherwise heavy built-ins, stone-clad walls, a raised planter deck or the overhead tank. Weight and height are the vocabulary the south-west understands.

Element corrections

Where geometry cannot move — a kitchen in the wrong zone, a toilet in the north-east — we apply the classical element balancing: earth and fire colours, metal placement, usage scheduling. Honest practitioners apply these after geometry is exhausted, and we say so plainly.

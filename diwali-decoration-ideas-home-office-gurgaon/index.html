Premium Diwali decoration is editing, not shopping.

Diwali is the one week of the year when your home or office is truly on display. Doors stay open, guests arrive unannounced, and every corner is judged in warm light. This guide shows how a design studio stages a space for the season, room by room, and what to fix first if the building itself is letting the decor down.

01 · The Principles What stylists actually do

Five rules behind a home that looks styled, not decorated.

Everything on this page comes back to these five moves. Master them and any room works.

01

Anchor each room with one statement

A brass figure on a console, a tall vase of tuberose, one framed artwork over the sofa. The eye needs a place to land first. Everything else supports it.

02

Work in three heights

Something tall, something mid-height, something low. A vase with branches, a sculpture, a tray of diyas. Flat arrangements always look unfinished.

03

Symmetry at the door, asymmetry inside

Twin lanterns or diya stands flanking the entrance signal ceremony. Inside, a slightly uneven grouping feels relaxed and lived-in.

04

Respect the surface

A centrepiece should take a third to half of the table and never block sightlines. A tiny showpiece disappears on a long dining table.

05

Mix two or three textures

Polished brass against matte ceramic. Silk under rough terracotta. Two or three finishes feel rich. Six feel chaotic.

Light is the soul of the festival

Layer the light, then dim the ceiling.

Premium Diwali rooms rarely use the main light at all. Start with ambient: dimmed ceiling or cove light. Add accent: a warm LED wash over one textured wall or artwork. Finish with flame: diyas, tealights and pillar candles at different heights. Keep every bulb at one warm temperature, and the room glows instead of glares.

  • Clay diyas grouped on a brass tray, seven to nine at a time
  • Diyas and candles in glass hurricanes on balconies and entrances
  • Warm-white fairy light on railings, never multicolour
  • One scented layer, incense or fresh flowers, never both at full strength

02 · Room by Room Home edition

The five rooms your guests will actually judge.

Skip the bedroom. These are the spaces Diwali guests see, in the order they see them.

01

The entrance

Lakshmi enters here, and so does every opinion of your home. One toran, twin lanterns or diya stands, and a styled console with a tall vase and a low brass tray. A petal rangoli sized to the doorway. Nothing more.

02

The living room

Three focal points: coffee table, console, one wall. A tray of candles and a small sculpture on the table. Jewel-toned silk cushions on the sofa. One framed artwork, not a wall of frames.

03

The pooja room

Restraint reads as reverence. A carved jaali backdrop, diyas in odd numbers at different heights, fresh jasmine and marigold for fragrance. No unrelated showpieces near the altar.

04

The dining table

A runner first, then a long, low centrepiece of marigolds between two brass candle stands, low enough to talk across. Mithai on a statement platter becomes part of the tablescape.

05

The balcony

Your home's face to the street. Warm-white lights on the railing, lanterns grouped in a corner, potted marigolds. A small sculpture lit from below makes a striking silhouette after dark.

Office edition

Your office gets judged more harshly than your home.

Guests forgive a home. Clients, employees and partners do not forgive an office. The reception is your toran moment. The facade is your rangoli. And unlike a home, an office shows its age in things decor cannot hide: tired paint at eye level, a flickering entrance light, worn stone at the threshold, a cluttered reception desk.

  • Reception: one statement console, fresh orchids, warm brass lighting
  • Facade: entrance lighting washed warm, lanterns at the door, no string-light clutter
  • Work floors: keep it minimal, a single festive accent per zone
  • Fix first: paint, entrance lighting and stone take days, not weeks

03 · The Fix-First List Before you buy a single diya

What decor cannot hide, and we can fix in weeks.

These are the fixes our studio runs every year in the weeks before Diwali, when families and offices want the building itself to match the styling.

01

Entrance lighting that flatters

A harsh white tube at the door ruins the warmest decor. A warm wash over the facade and entrance takes a day of electrical work and changes every photograph.

02

Paint refresh at eye level

Full repaints are off the table this close to the festival. Scuff-prone zones, doors, trims and the entrance wall can be done room by room in days.

03

Stone and marble polishing

Floors and counters photograph dull until they are polished. It is a two-day job with the right crew, and the shine holds through the wedding season.

04

Joinery that swallows clutter

A reception or foyer console with closed storage ends visual noise instantly. Custom pieces need two to three weeks, which is exactly why the call should happen now.

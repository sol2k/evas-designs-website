Where the money goes

The line items that decide your final number.

Across our full-home interior projects in Delhi NCR, joinery and kitchens take 35 to 45 percent of the budget, civil and false ceiling work 20 to 25 percent, electrical and lighting 10 to 15 percent, loose furniture and styling 15 to 20 percent, and design fees 8 to 15 percent where charged separately. The single biggest price lever is joinery specification, which is why our quotes name the material and hardware of every wardrobe and kitchen unit.

02 · Room by room Where rooms actually spend

Kitchens and bathrooms set the budget.

Rough working ranges per room, from hundreds of live-site bills across Delhi NCR.

01

Modular kitchen: Rs 3 to 8 lakh

Carcass material, shutter finish, hardware brand and appliances decide the number. A well-built mid-range kitchen outlasts a cheap premium-looking one by a decade.

02

Bathroom: Rs 1.5 to 4 lakh

Waterproofing, plumbing rework, sanitaryware and tiling, in that order of importance. The invisible layers are where leaks and rework live.

03

Wardrobes and joinery: Rs 1.5 to 4 lakh per bedroom

Material, hardware and internal fittings decide the price more than size. Our quotes name each so you can compare like with like.

04

Living and dining: Rs 4 to 12 lakh

False ceilings, panelling, lighting and loose furniture. This is where the visible luxury lives, and where phasing can spread cost most sensibly.

For full-home interiors, a realistic 2026 working range is Rs 1,200 to Rs 2,800 per sq ft of carpet area for standard-to-premium work, and Rs 2,800 to Rs 5,500 plus per sq ft for fully customised luxury interiors. A 1,800 sq ft apartment therefore typically lands between Rs 22 lakh and Rs 55 lakh all-in, depending on specification. Design fees alone, charged separately, usually run Rs 75 to Rs 250 per sq ft or 8 to 15 percent of the fit-out budget.

A proper scope covers space planning, working drawings, 3D views, a material and finish schedule, lighting design, custom furniture design, procurement support and site supervision until handover. The fee pays for the thousand decisions between a floor plan and a finished room: what the joinery detail looks like, which hinge, which grout colour, where the switch sits. That is where budgets are saved or lost.

Both models exist in Delhi NCR. Per-sq-ft pricing on carpet area is common for design-and-execute packages and is easier to budget against. Percentage-of-project-cost, typically 8 to 15 percent, is common when the designer only designs and you execute separately. Design-build studios like Evas Designs often fold design into the overall package, so you get one accountable party instead of separate bills from designer and contractor.

Kitchens and master bathrooms, always. A modular kitchen with good hardware and appliances runs Rs 3 to Rs 8 lakh and beyond, and a bathroom with proper waterproofing, sanitaryware and tiling runs Rs 1.5 to Rs 4 lakh. Living rooms look expensive but mostly spend on a few big-ticket items; kitchens and bathrooms spend on hundreds of invisible ones.

A sensible sequence is kitchen and bathrooms first, living areas next, bedrooms last, with wiring and plumbing future-proofed for the later phases in the first round. Phasing adds a little repetition cost, but it keeps quality consistent and avoids ripping up finished work later.

Design takes 4 to 8 weeks from first visit to frozen drawings, and execution runs 12 to 20 weeks for a full home depending on joinery load. A kitchen and two bathrooms alone typically take 6 to 8 weeks. Timelines slip most often on material decisions, which is why selections are frozen at design stage, not on site.

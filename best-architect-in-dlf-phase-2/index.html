01 · The Questions Ask these before you hire anyone

Six questions that sort real architects from good salesmen.

Ask them to us, ask them to anyone else. A studio worth hiring answers all six without hesitating.

01

"Will you walk my property before quoting?"

If the answer is a number instead of a date, end the call. No honest figure exists before someone has seen the columns, the plumbing stacks and the plot.

02

"What does my sanction actually allow?"

Floor area, setbacks, height. The gap between what owners assume and what papers say is where Phase 2 projects go wrong. Ask for it in writing.

03

"Can this frame carry a floor, and how do you know?"

Not vibes, a method: column sizes, foundation type, a structural engineer's sign-off. If the explanation takes one sentence, it is a guess.

04

"Who deals with the RWA and the utility paperwork?"

Phase 2 projects live or die on liaison. You want a named person, not "we handle all that." Ask who, and how often they have done it here.

05

"Show me one finished project I can walk through."

Rendered images prove rendering skill. A completed floor, a handed-over renovation, a reference who answers the phone: that is the portfolio.

06

"What happens when the budget slips?"

It will, somewhere. The honest answer is an itemised budget, a weekly site report, and a change-order process you sign before work starts.

03 · The Mistakes The expensive ones

Four ways Phase 2 projects go quietly wrong.

We have been called in after each of these. All four were avoidable for less than the cost of fixing them.

01

Buying a floor without reading the column grid

That odd notch in the living room is often a column you cannot move. Before you buy, have an architect read the structural drawing, not just the floor plan.

02

Adding a storey on a frame that was never designed for one

The most common call we get in Phase 2. Retrofitting a vertical addition onto an under-designed nineties frame costs multiples of doing it right the first time.

03

Starting society-regulated work without the NOC

A renovation halted mid-demo by an RWA notice loses months and money. The NOC takes days to obtain and is always cheaper than the notice.

04

Vastu corrections drawn after the structure is cast

A displaced kitchen or entrance designed in on paper costs nothing. Chiselled into a finished slab, it costs lakhs. Decide orientation before the first column.

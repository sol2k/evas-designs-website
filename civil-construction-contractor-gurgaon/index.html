02 · What decides cost & quality The six real variables

Where civil budgets are won or lost.

Anyone can pour concrete. The difference between a structure that lasts fifty years and one that starts complaining in five is decided by these six variables — all of which appear by name in our BOQ.

  • Soil and foundation type. Gurgaon spans dense soil in old sectors to loose alluvium near the expressway. Raft, isolated footings or piles change foundation cost by 50 percent or more; a soil test before design is non-negotiable on plots.
  • Concrete grade. M25 is the residential floor minimum we work to; higher grades or special mixes for long spans cost more and save far more in cracking and callbacks.
  • Steel brand and detailing. TATA, SAIL and Jindal HYQ are what we use. The detailing — lap lengths, stirrup spacing, cover blocks — decides seismic behaviour, and it is where cheap structure work quietly cuts corners.
  • Height and access. Stilt-plus-three needs more scaffolding, material hoisting and safety systems than a single storey. Narrow plotted lanes in old Gurgaon colonies add logistics cost that honest quotes show separately.
  • Season and curing. Concrete poured in peak May heat or January cold needs curing discipline and sometimes admixtures. Monsoon masonry needs dry blocks and slow mortar; shortcuts here surface as efflorescence and damp within one season.
  • Who supervises. A structure site without daily engineering supervision is where cover blocks disappear and extra water gets added to the mix. Our sites carry a dedicated engineer, and you get photo reports of every pour.

Quality checks you can verify

  • Concrete cube tests every pour, third-party lab reports shared
  • Cover blocks at every reinforcement layer, photographed before shuttering closes
  • Needle vibrator compaction with a written pour log: time, weather, crew
  • Seven-day wet curing with daily photo evidence
  • Steel billed on actual weighed quantities against the drawing bar-bending schedule
  • Brickwork to plumb with chicken mesh at every concrete-masonry joint

Quoted separately, upfront

  • Soil testing and geotechnical reports (we arrange, third party)
  • Sanction drawings, liaisoning and approval fees
  • Structural steel quantities beyond drawing estimates, at booked rates
  • GST at 18 percent on the final invoice

Approvals and paperwork

Structure work rides on paperwork: sanctioned drawings, RERA registration where applicable, and completion or occupancy certificates at the end. We coordinate drawings, structural engineers and approvals as part of turnkey scopes, and we flag paperwork gaps before the first excavation cut, not after the slab. 03 · Fit Civil-only or turnkey

Three ways owners engage us for civil work.

The right scope depends on where your project stands and how much coordination you want to carry.

Structure-only civil

You hold the architecture and interiors elsewhere; we execute the shell to your engineer's drawings with our crews, testing and BOQ discipline. Common for stilt-plus-floor projects where the family architect leads design.

Design-build turnkey

One contract for architecture, structure and finishes. The route we recommend for most owners: one accountable team, no design-execution gaps, and typically 8 to 12 percent cheaper than split contracts because supervision, scaffolding and rework are eliminated, not re-billed.

Rescue and mid-project takeover

Stalled sites, contractor exits, quality disputes. We start with an independent structural audit, give you a written rectification and completion plan, then execute it. About a fifth of our civil work arrives mid-story; every one of them wishes it had arrived sooner.

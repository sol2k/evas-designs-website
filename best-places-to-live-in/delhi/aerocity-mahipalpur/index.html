Living by the airport: Aerocity and Mahipalpur, honestly.

No Delhi address divides opinion like the airport belt. For airline crews, consultants and weekly flyers, Aerocity and Mahipalpur deliver fifteen-minute terminals and hotel-grade services at rents far below South Delhi purchase prices. For everyone else, the noise, the leasehold paperwork and the flyover skyline argue back. This is the honest guide: who should live here, what it really costs, and where the traps are.

The renovation lever

Tired stock in every one of these bands trades at a 20 to 35 percent discount to rebuilt or new stock, and a full renovation costs 15 to 20 percent of the new price. Buy tired, renovate well, hold long: it is the most repeatable arbitrage in Delhi NCR housing.

Interiors are the second budget

Whatever you buy, the home you live in is the purchase plus the interior. Budget Rs 2,150 to 3,000 per sq ft for a full premium interior or Rs 950 to 1,400 for a selective upgrade, and review the floor plan before you sign, not after.

Rent first, always

The belt's economics reward flexibility. Take a twelve-month serviced rental, learn your noise exposure and building quality, and only then consider anything longer.

Acoustic and blackout budgets

The signature airport-belt scope is acoustic glazing and full blackout drapery, typically Rs 2 to 4 lakh for a 3BHK. Without them, the location's promise never lands.

07 Asked on every airport-belt visit

Your questions, answered plainly.

Both, by design. The district master plan splits Aerocity into hospitality and commercial districts with a limited residential component, mostly serviced apartments and hotel-residences. Most people who live in the belt actually live in the surrounding pockets: Mahipalpur village edge, Rangpuri and the NH-8 service road apartments that predate the district.

Rents run Rs 45,000 to 90,000 per month for furnished three bedroom apartments in Aerocity towers, and Rs 25,000 to 50,000 in the NH-8 pockets. Purchase is the complicated part: much of the belt is L&DO leasehold with transfer restrictions, so outright ownership is rare. Where freehold-equivalent purchase exists, rates run Rs 14,000 to 22,000 per sq ft.

For most people, rent. The belt rewards flexibility: careers move, flight patterns change, and the leasehold structure muddies resale. Buying makes sense only for genuine airport-anchored lives, typically airline and aviation families, and only in pockets with clean title. Even then, compare against Vasant Kunj ten minutes east, where ownership is simpler.

Three honest ones: aircraft noise on specific approach paths, a flyover-dominated streetscape with limited walkability, and the service-apartment character of much of the stock, which means higher turnover neighbours. The airport-adjacent parts of Vasant Kunj and Mahipalpur get most of the benefit with fewer of the costs.

Apartments here are typically newer, so scopes skew toward furnishing and selective upgrades: Rs 950 to 1,400 per sq ft for a selective upgrade and Rs 1,800 to 2,600 per sq ft for a full furnished finish, with acoustic windows and blackout systems as the signature airport-belt line items.

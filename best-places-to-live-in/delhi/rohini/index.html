The renovation lever

Tired stock in every one of these bands trades at a 20 to 35 percent discount to rebuilt or new stock, and a full renovation costs 15 to 20 percent of the new price. Buy tired, renovate well, hold long: it is the most repeatable arbitrage in Delhi NCR housing.

Interiors are the second budget

Whatever you buy, the home you live in is the purchase plus the interior. Budget Rs 2,150 to 3,000 per sq ft for a full premium interior or Rs 950 to 1,400 for a selective upgrade, and review the floor plan before you sign, not after.

The DDA-plus-renovation math

A settled-sector DDA flat bought at Rs 1.8 crore and renovated for Rs 25 lakh out-performs tired stock by 20 to 30 percent on resale, with the finishes chosen by you.

Against Dwarka

The two planned sub-cities split the value market: Dwarka wins on metro density and Gurgaon access, Rohini on price and North-West school belts.

Japanese Park premium

Sectors within walking distance of the park hold a measurable premium that has never reversed. If green matters to your mornings, pay it.

07 Asked on every Rohini visit

Your questions, answered plainly.

DDA flats start around Rs 1 to 1.6 crore for two bedroom units and Rs 1.6 to 2.2 crore for three bedroom flats in settled sectors. Cooperative group housing runs Rs 2 to 3 crore for larger formats, and newly built builder floors in sectors 8, 9, 16 and 24 command Rs 2.5 to 4 crore, with premium corner and wide-road floors crossing Rs 6 crore.

Sectors 7 to 9 and 13 to 19 form the settled core: working markets, metro proximity and mature communities. Sectors 24 to 37 skew newer, with more builder-floor stock and wider roads at a premium. The Japanese Park side holds green cover that the older sectors never had.

The Red Line stations at Rithala, Rohini West and Pitampura cover the southern sectors, and the Outer Ring Road links the belt to North and Central Delhi. Gurgaon commutes are long by any route, which is why Rohini buyers typically work in North, Central or West Delhi, or along the Ring Road corridor.

DDA flats from the 1980s and 1990s renew well: Rs 1,000 to 1,600 per sq ft comprehensively, typically Rs 18 to 35 lakh for a 3BHK. Builder floors from the 2000s usually need selective upgrades at Rs 950 to 1,400 per sq ft. The value case is the strongest in Delhi: renovated Rohini stock outperforms tired stock by 20 to 30 percent.

The planned-sector format works: lit internal roads, active RWAs in settled sectors, schools including DPS Rohini and Mother Mary's inside the belt, and the Japanese Park for weekends. Like the rest of Delhi, street experience varies block by block, and the settled core sectors consistently rate the highest.

DDA flats win on location value per rupee and take renovations beautifully. Builder floors give modern specifications, stilt parking and an easier day one, at a 30 to 50 percent premium. If you can wait sixteen weeks for a renovation, the DDA-plus-renovation arithmetic is hard to beat.

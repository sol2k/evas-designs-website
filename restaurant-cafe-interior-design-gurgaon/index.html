Why all-in runs 2 to 3 times the interior

Kitchen equipment and stainless fabrication take 15 to 25 percent of the project, exhaust and make-up air 5 to 10, HVAC 8 to 15, fire and gas systems 3 to 8, and loose furniture, signage, licences and pre-opening another 15 to 25. The interior fit-out is only 35 to 50 percent of what you will actually spend before the first bill prints.

Worked example, 2,000 sq ft casual dining

Interiors at mid-range: Rs 28 to 44 lakh. Seating at 1.3 to 1.7 sq m per diner lands 70 to 90 covers. Kitchen, exhaust, HVAC and fire add Rs 25 to 55 lakh. All-in: Rs 55 lakh to 1.25 crore, or roughly Rs 60,000 to 1.9 lakh per seat, excluding deposits and working capital.

The per-seat lens

Operators think in cost per seat because revenue arrives per seat. Banquette-heavy plans seat more per square metre than loose four-tops, which is why the layout is a financial document, not just a drawing. We model seats, spend and revenue per sq ft together before the concept freezes.

Mall versus high street in Gurgaon

Mall units (Galleria, 32nd Avenue, Airia and the Golf Course Road malls) arrive as warm shells with strict fit-out manuals, night-work rules and facade controls, but services in place. High-street Sector 29 or Golf Course Extension units give design freedom and signage visibility, and demand more services work. We budget the two differently from the first meeting.

The kitchen decides the plan

Fix the menu before the layout, always. The kitchen takes 30 to 40 percent of the unit, and its exhaust route, gas bank, grease trap and washing geometry dictate where dining can go. Concepts that lock the dining mood-board first pay for the mistake in ducting and lost covers.

Licences shape the layout

FSSAI expects hygienic zoning and washable surfaces; the fire NOC for larger formats drives exit widths, extinguisher and alarm layouts; excise layouts for bars are their own discipline. We draw to these codes from day one, so the licence inspection is a formality, not a redesign.
